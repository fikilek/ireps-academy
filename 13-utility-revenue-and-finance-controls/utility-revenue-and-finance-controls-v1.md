# Utility Revenue and Finance Controls V1

## New Knowledge System: Utility Revenue and Finance Controls

This chapter will explain how utilities make money, lose money, and protect money.

Utility revenue is not only a finance office issue. It is also connected to fieldwork, meters, premises, customer data, inspections, evidence, and good operational controls.

iREPS must help users understand that every meter, every reading, every inspection, and every piece of field evidence can affect utility revenue.

---

## 1. How meters generate revenue

Meters are one of the main ways utilities measure usage and generate revenue.

This section will cover:

- why meters matter to utility income
- how meter readings become billing data
- conventional meters
- prepaid meters
- estimated billing risk
- missed readings
- wrong meter numbers
- wrong premise-meter links

---

## 2. How utilities lose revenue

Utilities can lose revenue when usage is not correctly measured, billed, collected, or protected.

This section will cover:

- electricity theft
- water theft
- meter bridging
- bypassed meters
- illegal connections
- tampered meters
- disconnected meters that are still being used
- water leaks
- burst pipes
- unmetered consumption
- faulty meters
- poor meter reading
- wrong customer/premise data
- weak field evidence
- delayed inspections

---

## 3. Revenue protection

Revenue protection is the work done to find, prevent, and reduce utility revenue losses.

This section will cover:

- meter audits
- meter inspections
- meter replacement
- disconnection and reconnection control
- exception reporting
- field evidence review
- supervisor checks
- customer data cleansing
- premise-meter verification
- loss investigation

---

## 4. Finance controls

Finance controls help protect utility revenue and reduce the risk of errors, fraud, weak approvals, and poor billing decisions.

This section will cover:

- separation of duties
- approval workflows
- audit trails
- evidence-based decisions
- exception queues
- reconciliation between field data and billing data
- monitoring abnormal consumption
- tracking zero consumption
- tracking negative consumption
- tracking high consumption
- tracking repeated No Access
- tracking disconnected-but-consuming cases

---

## 5. Best practice

Good utility revenue control depends on clean data, good fieldwork, strong supervision, and useful reporting.

This section will cover:

- keep meter records clean
- confirm the correct premise
- confirm the correct meter number
- take clear media evidence
- investigate abnormal readings early
- protect the meter-to-premise link
- do regular meter audits
- use reports to find revenue leakage
- make supervisors review risky cases
